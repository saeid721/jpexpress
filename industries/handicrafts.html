UNDERSTAND YOUR LOGISTICS NEEDS

Built around the way handicraft businesses ship

Handicraft shipments vary widely — a single sample piece, a curated collection for a trade show, or a large mixed carton of home décor and gift products. Unlike uniform factory goods, handicrafts often have irregular shapes, delicate finishes, natural materials and handmade variation. Each shipment needs assessment around its contents, fragility, packaging requirement, destination and intended delivery schedule.

JP Express helps you review suitable courier, air-freight or sea-freight options based on the shipment details you provide. Service selection and requirements should be confirmed for the exact product, route and destination.

COMMON CHALLENGES

Plan for the details that matter

Shipment requirements vary. These are the considerations handicraft makers and exporters most often ask about.

01

Fragile and irregular shapes

Handmade pieces often have delicate edges, protrusions or non-standard shapes. Internal cushioning and dividers are essential to prevent damage in transit.

02

Natural materials and destination rules

Some destinations apply additional checks on wood, natural fibre, shell, bone or mixed-material handicrafts. Accurate content description reduces the risk of delays.

03

Sample-to-bulk scale variability

A single artisan sample may move by courier, while a full collection or trade-show consignment needs air or sea freight. Each shipment needs its own assessment.

04

Volumetric weight vs. actual weight

Lightweight but bulky handicrafts often pay by volumetric weight, not actual weight. Carton dimensions materially affect cost for this category.

PACKAGING & PREPARATION

Fragile-safe packing for handmade goods

  • Wrap each piece individually and cushion fragile edges or protrusions.
  • Use dividers so products don't touch or shift inside the carton.
  • Choose carton strength based on the item's weight, shape and fragility — not just its size.
  • Support tall or irregular pieces internally to prevent tipping or bending.
  • Describe materials accurately (wood, fibre, metal, shell, etc.) and confirm destination restrictions.

RESTRICTED ITEMS & COMPLIANCE

Declare contents accurately

Possible considerations for handicrafts

Natural materials — wood, cane, bamboo, bone, shell, natural fibre or animal-derived components — may face additional checks at some destinations. Items containing plant material, seeds or untreated wood can be subject to specific import rules. A clear product description with accurate material details and HS code reduces the risk of delays. Confirm destination-specific rules before dispatch.

COST & TRANSIT

Understand what affects your shipment

Shipping cost factors

Rates depend on actual and volumetric weight, carton dimensions, quantity, destination, service level, packaging and freight mode. Handicrafts are frequently lightweight but bulky — volumetric weight often dominates the cost. Accurate carton dimensions help produce a realistic quote, and fragile or oversized items may need additional packing assessment.

Transit considerations

Handmade production and custom orders can have variable completion dates. Build in packing time and confirm transit estimates for the chosen service and destination. Air freight suits small, time-sensitive shipments; sea freight is usually more economical for larger collections that are not urgent.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Final service acceptance, documents and transit estimates depend on the specific shipment and destination.

Can fragile handicrafts be shipped internationally?

Suitability depends on the item, materials, packaging, destination and available service. Share photographs or a detailed description of the piece when requesting an assessment, and mention any delicate edges, glass, ceramic or fragile parts.

How should handmade products be packed for export?

Wrap each piece individually, cushion edges and protrusions, use dividers so items don't touch, support tall or irregular shapes internally, and select carton strength based on weight and fragility rather than size alone.

Can I send a single sample before a bulk order?

Sample-shipment options may be available depending on the item and destination. Describe the piece, its purpose (buyer approval, trade show, gallery submission, etc.) and the destination when requesting a quote.

Do material restrictions apply to handicrafts?

They can. Items made from wood, natural fibre, cane, bamboo, shell, bone or other natural materials may face additional checks at some destinations. Accurate material description and destination confirmation are important before dispatch.

Should I choose air freight or sea freight for handicrafts?

Air freight suits smaller, time-sensitive or trade-show shipments, while sea freight is usually more economical for larger collections that are not urgent. Handicrafts are often light but bulky, so volumetric weight can affect both cost and mode choice. Ask the team to assess both options.

Can recurring shipments to the same buyer or gallery be coordinated?

Yes. Share expected frequency, common destinations, typical carton dimensions and delivery windows to discuss a suitable business-shipping arrangement.
