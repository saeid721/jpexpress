How to prepare your first export shipment from Bangladesh

A clear, practical checklist for documents, packaging, customs and the decisions that make an international shipment run smoothly.

JP Express Editorial Team 18 Aug 2026 8 min read

On this page

    Starting your export journey from Bangladesh can feel overwhelming, but with the right preparation, it becomes a straightforward process. This guide walks you through everything you need to know before your first shipment leaves Dhaka.

    1. Understand Your Product Classification

    Every export product needs a Harmonized System (HS) code. This code determines:

    • Customs duties in the destination country
    • Any special permits or certifications required
    • Restrictions that may apply

    Your HS code must be accurate on all documentation. If you are unsure, consult with your freight forwarder or the Bangladesh Customs authority.

    2. Prepare Essential Documents

    Every export shipment requires these core documents:

    • Commercial Invoice: Details the transaction between buyer and seller, including product descriptions, quantities, values, and Incoterms.
    • Packing List: Itemizes the contents of each package, showing weights, dimensions, and package counts.
    • Certificate of Origin: Proves where the goods were manufactured.
    • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill: The transport document issued by the carrier.

    3. Choose the Right Incoterms

    Incoterms define who is responsible for what in the shipping process. For first-time exporters, common choices include:

    • FOB (Free On Board): You are responsible until goods are loaded on the vessel.
    • CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight): You cover cost, insurance, and freight to the destination port.
    • EXW (Ex Works): Buyer takes responsibility from your premises.

    4. Package for International Transit

    Your packaging must survive multiple handling points. Consider using export-grade boxes, protecting against moisture, labeling clearly with shipping marks, and testing for stacking strength.

    5. Verify Restricted Items

    Some products face restrictions or require special permits. Textiles may need GSP certificates, food products require health certificates, and electronics need compliance documentation.

    6. Arrange Logistics and Insurance

    Choose your shipping method based on urgency, volume, and value. Air freight suits time-sensitive goods, while sea freight saves cost for larger shipments.

    7. Work with a Reliable Freight Forwarder

    A good freight forwarder handles documentation, customs clearance, and logistics coordination. Look for experience with your product type and strong destination networks.

    Next Steps

    Once you have completed these preparations, you are ready to book your shipment. Start with a small trial shipment to test your processes before scaling up.
